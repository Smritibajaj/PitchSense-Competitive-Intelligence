# PitchSense AI — Phase 2: Competitor Website Messaging and Landing Page Analysis (Batch 2)

**Research date:** October 19, 2026 (Europe/Berlin). Pages captured directly from official websites via full-HTML capture; all three homepages were additionally inspected in a rendered browser.

**Scope:** Attention (attention.com — attention.tech now redirects), Backstory (backstory.ai, ex-People.ai), Outreach (outreach.ai) — homepages plus one CRO-relevant supporting page each. Phase 1 discovery, classification, and capability scoring are not repeated here. This batch covers the remaining high-priority competitors from the Phase 1 overlap analysis (Attention and Outreach in the top overlap tier; Backstory in the shared-memory and CRO five).

**Method and verification scope.** Heading levels were verified against actual HTML tags in the saved pages, not inferred. The checks actually performed: (1) exact-copy fields (H1s, hero paragraphs, Table B headings/subheadings, Table E claims) were re-checked against the page cited in the same row; (2) Table D CTA labels and destinations were checked against the same anchor element in the saved HTML using exact label and resolved-URL comparison (41 pairs checked, zero mismatches; fragment-only anchors compared as fragments); form buttons, rendered-only links, and unverified destinations are classified and labeled separately rather than audited; (3) Table F vendor-column quotations were checked against that vendor's pages — mixed-source clauses were checked against their specific cited pages, though this check is not comprehensive per-page validation. **Targeted rendered checks confirmed:** Attention's hero (H1, paragraph, email form, product-UI "next move" mockup) and major sections; Backstory's hero (H1, CTAs, "Connect Your Revenue Stack" graphic), solutions tabs, final CTA, and persistent "Ask Paige" chat widget; Outreach's hero (rendering nuance: the H1 renders as the eyebrow and the H2 as the visible main headline), logo wall, and CTAs. **Client-side content (verified in rendered page, not in saved HTML):** Attention's hero form placeholder, its Tracksuit testimonial, and its Sales Leaders/Sales Reps persona-tab panels (headings labeled "visible heading — HTML level unverified"); Backstory's chat-widget texts. **Explicitly not verified:** Attention's /book post-submit journey (page captured, nothing submitted); Backstory's hero "See how it works" beyond its page anchor; Outreach's hero video behavior (play button links to a page anchor); all conversion outcomes — CTA frequency is recorded as observed structure, not as evidence of effectiveness.

**Limitations.** (1) Table F remains PROVISIONAL pending the official PitchSense Product Narrative v1 (requested once in the batch-1 delivery; its four-intelligence structure was recovered from the original research brief preserved in session history, but the official document is still needed). (2) Backstory's homepage states two different adoption figures with different units — "TRUSTED BY 100,000+ Sellers AT TOP ORGANIZATIONS" (logo strip) and "Trusted by 500,000+ GTM teams..." (testimonials section) — both recorded verbatim; different units mean these are not directly comparable and no contradiction is claimed. (3) On Attention's rendered page, ordinary DOM queries returned no headings while the saved HTML source contains semantic heading tags; heading evidence comes from the HTML source, and rendered content was confirmed via screenshots/accessibility output (the cause of the query discrepancy was not established). (4) Outreach's H1/H2 rendering nuance recorded in Table A. (5) Demo figures in product-UI mockups (Attention's NORTHWIND deal, Backstory's simulated answers) are illustrative/simulated content, not verified customer outcomes. (6) Vendor claims (metrics, testimonials, analyst reports) are the vendors' own statements, preserved verbatim, not independently verified.

## Final Strategic Analysis

All statements below are analyst interpretation grounded in the tables above. Vendor claims are the vendors' own statements, preserved verbatim; they are not independently verified. CTA frequency is recorded, not treated as evidence of conversion effectiveness. No PitchSense landing-page copy is written here.

### 1. Dominant homepage narrative of each competitor

- **Attention** runs an execution narrative: "AI agents that work your pipeline while you sell" ([homepage](https://www.attention.com/)) — the product acts ("You approve. Attention executes."), and the page shows the acting (agent cards with done-state counters).
- **Backstory** runs an answers narrative: "Win more, even in complex selling motions" with "the backstory on every deal" ([homepage](https://www.backstory.ai/)) — positioned explicitly against data tools: "answers, not more data" (FAQ, on the People.ai rebrand).
- **Outreach** runs a capacity narrative: "You didn't hire enough sellers. Now you don't have to." ([homepage](https://www.outreach.ai/)) — AI agents as teammates that multiply seller capacity across every deal and account.

### 2. How each communicates business value before features

- **Attention** leads with outcomes and proves them with agent-behavior cards before any product grid; its platform section then walks through five capability modules each with its own content CTA ([homepage](https://www.attention.com/)).
- **Backstory** leads with value through the answers promise and a 3-step "question → reasoning → response" explainer that makes the answer pipeline itself the credibility device ([homepage](https://www.backstory.ai/)).
- **Outreach** leads with the capacity gap (hiring shortfall), then presents its six agentic-AI innovation highlights before quantifying consolidation value with the Amplitude case ($600K/year) ([homepage](https://www.outreach.ai/)).

### 3. Who communicates shared memory most effectively

Analyst judgment about communication, not measured effectiveness: **Backstory** is closest to a memory-first story — the brand IS memory ("the backstory on every deal"), with two years of deal history analyzed on day one ([homepage](https://www.backstory.ai/)). **Outreach** abstracts memory into the Knowledge card (uploaded battlecards/case studies informing AI agents) and MCP stack flow ([homepage](https://www.outreach.ai/)). **Attention** frames memory as agent learning ("AI agents that learn from your best sales conversations", page title) plus auditability ([homepage](https://www.attention.com/)). None of the three uses the phrase "shared memory" or "organizational memory" on the inspected pages.

### 4. Who most directly addresses CRO problems

**Backstory**, on coverage of the inspected pages: a dedicated [CRO role page](https://www.backstory.ai/role/executives) with a provocative H1 ("You're not getting the answers you need. And you know it."), a CRO persona card ("The Decision Maker"), a CRO-focused FAQ ("CROs and VPs of Sales who own the number and answer to the board when it changes"), and an interactive CRO answer demo with simulated source-cited answers ([homepage](https://www.backstory.ai/), [CRO page](https://www.backstory.ai/role/executives)). **Attention** is second — forecast-from-buyers-said messaging, market-cap/jobs-at-risk framing in its Revenue Teams tab (illustrative demo content), and a sales-leaders page with a 5%-forecast-error testimonial ([homepage](https://www.attention.com/), [sales-leaders page](https://www.attention.com/solutions/sales-leaders)). **Outreach** is third on CRO specificity: strong forecast-plan page ([forecast-plan](https://www.outreach.ai/solutions/forecast-plan)) but the homepage speaks to capacity and full-funnel execution rather than the CRO's number ([homepage](https://www.outreach.ai/)).

### 5. Common CTA patterns

- Demo-anchored CTAs appear on all three ("Get a demo" / "Book a demo" / "Schedule a demo" / "Request a demo") — consistent with the category default ([Attention](https://www.attention.com/), [Backstory](https://www.backstory.ai/), [Outreach](https://www.outreach.ai/)).
- **Backstory shows the most self-serve entry points**: "Guide me" (nav), a revenue-assessment quiz banner, "Watch Instant Demo" and "Read AI Buying Guide" as final CTAs, and a "Take The Tour" on-page demo anchor on the CRO page ([homepage](https://www.backstory.ai/), [CRO page](https://www.backstory.ai/role/executives)).
- **Attention shows email lead-capture forms** in hero, mid-page, and final position ("Get started"), with a /book page captured but its post-submit journey unverified — conversion intent beyond email capture is not claimed ([homepage](https://www.attention.com/)).
- **Outreach shows pricing transparency**: "Pricing" in nav and "View pricing info" in the final CTA ([homepage](https://www.outreach.ai/)).
- Content-led CTAs ("Discover X" / "Learn more") are used heavily by Attention and Outreach to route visitors to product pages ([Attention](https://www.attention.com/), [Outreach](https://www.outreach.ai/)).

### 6. Storytelling strengths to learn from

- **Attention**: the "done-state counter" device (each agent card shows the action completed: "3 FOLLOW-UPS SENT", "PROPOSAL SENT") and the direct "us vs LEGACY" comparison table ([homepage](https://www.attention.com/)).
- **Backstory**: the strongest objection-handling FAQ observed across both batches — 13 questions pre-empting "why not just CRM + LLM", build-vs-buy, AI cost, and the People.ai rebrand ([homepage](https://www.backstory.ai/)).
- **Outreach**: quantified consolidation storytelling (Amplitude: five platforms to one, $600K/year) and a full footer ecosystem of comparison pages, persona pages, and free calculators/quizzes ([homepage](https://www.outreach.ai/)).

### 7. Messaging opportunities for PitchSense to investigate (updated against batch-1 hypotheses)

Batch 2 materially weakens three of batch 1's whitespace hypotheses — these are now testing questions, not open territory:

1. **Content intelligence (revised).** Batch 1 hypothesized content intelligence as homepage white space. Batch 2 shows adjacent claims: Attention's Collateral Genie and Content Gaps agents ([homepage](https://www.attention.com/)) and Outreach's Knowledge card (battlecards/case studies uploaded to inform AI agents, [homepage](https://www.outreach.ai/)). The untested nuance for PitchSense: none of the six inspected pages claims APPROVED-content governance (curated, compliance-safe content libraries tied to deal outcomes). Test the governance angle specifically, not content intelligence generically.
2. **Source-backed AI answers (revised).** Batch 1 hypothesized citation-grounded answers as unclaimed. Batch 2 shows Attention's "FOUND SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE" and "SOURCE: GOOGLE MEET + VIEW RECORDING" demo UI ([homepage](https://www.attention.com/)) and Backstory's "defensible enough to share with leadership" with sourced answers ([homepage](https://www.backstory.ai/)). The differentiator left to test is the DEPTH of source-linking (every insight linkable to its underlying call/email) as a hero promise rather than a demo detail.
3. **A memory-first hero (unchanged).** None of the six inspected pages (across both batches) leads its hero with an explicit organizational-memory promise; Backstory's "backstory" branding is the closest. The hypothesis survives batch 2 but Backstory is the competitive benchmark to beat.
4. **AI roleplay / practice (unchanged).** Not featured on any of the six inspected homepages. Verify against roleplay specialists from Phase 1 before claiming whitespace.
5. **CRO-answer demos (new).** Backstory's interactive CRO answer demo with simulated source citations is a conversion pattern worth testing for PitchSense: it demonstrates the product's core value (answers with sources) without a meeting.
