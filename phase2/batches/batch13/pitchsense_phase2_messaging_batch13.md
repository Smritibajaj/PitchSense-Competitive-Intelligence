# PitchSense AI — Phase 2: Competitor Website Messaging and Landing Page Analysis (Batch 13)

**Research date:** October 20, 2026 (Europe/Berlin). Added in the Oct 20 scope swap (replacing six weak-fit companies). Homepages were captured as HTML in the light format, then analysed in the full format the same day, with one CRO-relevant supporting page per company.

**Scope:** Oliv.ai (https://www.oliv.ai/; CRO page: https://www.oliv.ai/oliv-forecast); Glean (https://www.glean.com/; CRO page: https://www.glean.com/solutions/departments/sales); Spekit (https://www.spekit.com/; CRO page: https://www.spekit.com/solutions/chief-revenue-officers). Phase 1 discovery, classification and capability scoring are not repeated here.

**Method and verification scope.** Heading levels were verified against HTML tags in the saved pages, in document order; nav, mega-menu, footer, tab and responsive duplicates were excluded. Every exact-copy field (Table A hero fields, Table B headings and subheadings, Table D CTA labels, Table E claims and the quoted text in Table F) was string-matched against the saved captures after normalising whitespace, HTML entities and curly quotes; values split across inline tags were matched word by word and are noted in their rows. CTA destinations are the `href` in the HTML; destinations that were not fetched are marked "intent unverified". All captures are in `captures/` in this folder.

**Limitations.** (1) HTML only: no rendered-browser check was possible for this batch, so visual hero media, client-side rotating headlines and script-rendered content are labelled as such rather than confirmed. (2) Table A hero fields equal the light-format row (`website_messaging_batch13.csv`); Not-verified light fields were re-checked the same day and any field filled is noted in that row's Verification cell as "Gap-fill". (3) Table F was written directly against PitchSense Product Narrative v1 (Sep 30, 2026); its PitchSense column is copied verbatim from batches 1–3. (4) Vendor metrics, testimonials and badges are vendor claims, recorded verbatim and not verified. Messaging emphasis is not evidence that a capability is missing.

**Canonical data:** the `table_*.csv` files in this folder. This report renders them; if a table changes, re-render the table sections.

## Final Strategic Analysis

All statements below are analyst interpretation grounded in the tables above. Vendor claims are the vendors' own statements, preserved verbatim; they are not independently verified. CTA frequency is recorded, not treated as evidence of conversion effectiveness. No PitchSense landing-page copy is written here.

### 1. Dominant homepage narrative of each competitor

- **Oliv.ai** runs a replacement narrative: "Your Revenue Org, Powered by Autonomous Agents" ([homepage](https://www.oliv.ai/)). The page argues that point tools trap budget ("Budget locked in SaaS"), offers the same apps for less ("Same Apps at 70% Less") and says the savings fund agents that work from one context layer.
- **Glean** runs a context-and-cost narrative: "Complete context that makes AI work at enterprise scale." ([homepage](https://www.glean.com/)). Its problem section is AI spend, not a business function: "AI adoption is accelerating. AI costs are accelerating faster. Glean is built for enterprise scale." Sales is one department among many.
- **Spekit** runs a readiness narrative for a mixed human and agent workforce: "AI-first revenue enablement for every rep and every agent" ([homepage](https://www.spekit.com/)), built on "One governed source of GTM truth. Every Rep. Every Agent."

### 2. How each communicates business value before features

- **Oliv.ai** shows features first (the "Agents for entire revenue lifecycle" team tabs follow the logo strip), then makes the value case in money: the AI-maturity ladder ("But teams are stuck at Level 2 AI adoption."), the cost problem and a per-seat price table against Gong, Highspot, Salesloft, Clari and Gainsight ([homepage](https://www.oliv.ai/)).
- **Glean** is the most value-first of the three. After the hero and logos it states the cost problem, then three value lines ("Better answers. Built on what your business knows."; "Fewer tokens."; "Lower cost to serve.") before security, statistics and the platform overview ([homepage](https://www.glean.com/)).
- **Spekit** puts a component list in the hero ("content management, learning, coaching, content creation, digital sales rooms and deal intelligence") and the four pillars next, then turns to outcomes with "Faster ramp, higher capacity, stronger wins." and three customer metrics ([homepage](https://www.spekit.com/)). Its outcome framing returns at the close: "See how Spekit increases revenue per rep through faster ramp, more capacity, faster change adoption, and buyer experiences that move deals."

### 3. Who communicates shared memory most effectively

Analyst judgment about communication, not measured effectiveness: **Oliv.ai** has the strongest shared-context story observed in any batch so far. It walks the reader through the full argument in sequence: context is scattered ("Every tool holds a piece of context: meetings in Gong, usage in Pendo, Tickets in Zendesk."), Oliv captures it ("Your calls, emails, Zoom, messages, and even in-person meetings"), maps it ("Your agents know who said what, and which account or opportunity it belongs to."), prepares it in advance ("Oliv prepares the context before your AI needs it") and shares it with other agents ("Use MCP to give your agents access to the context Oliv builds for your accounts and opportunities.") ([homepage](https://www.oliv.ai/)). **Glean** makes context its hero claim but scopes it to the whole company rather than deals ([homepage](https://www.glean.com/)). **Spekit** describes a shared source of approved knowledge rather than a memory of what happened in deals: "Spekit grounds every rep and every agent in the same governed source of truth" ([CRO page](https://www.spekit.com/solutions/chief-revenue-officers)). None of the three uses memory as its headline word on the inspected pages; all three say context.

### 4. Who most directly addresses CRO problems

**Spekit**, on coverage of the inspected pages: a dedicated [CRO page](https://www.spekit.com/solutions/chief-revenue-officers) with the H1 "Unlock more revenue per rep", a change-velocity problem framed for the CRO ("Your product changed this week. Did your reps? Did your agents?"), a loss mechanism ("every stale answer, whether a rep gives it or an agent does, costs you a deal."), a CRO testimonial and a CRO FAQ that reaches the forecast ("which deals are at risk before the forecast call"). **Oliv.ai** is a close second and the strongest on the forecast itself: its [Forecast page](https://www.oliv.ai/oliv-forecast) promises "At-risk deals flagged before the review call", an agent that "reads every deal scored, drafts the rep's submission, and updates you" and a number with explanations ("Leadership sees the number with the reasons attached."); it does not address the CRO by title on the inspected pages. **Glean** is third: its [Sales page](https://www.glean.com/solutions/departments/sales) speaks to sales cycles, ramp and deal-team alignment, but the homepage speaks to enterprise AI cost and the CRO is not named.

### 5. Common CTA patterns

- Demo or meeting CTAs appear on all three, with different weight. Spekit routes seven Table D rows to /demo-request ("Book a Demo" in nav, hero, outcome cards, final CTA and footer, plus "Get Started" and "Get In Touch") ([homepage](https://www.spekit.com/)). Glean has a single conversion path, "Get a demo", in header, hero and final CTA ([homepage](https://www.glean.com/)). Oliv.ai frames the meeting as short and founder-led ("Book a 7m Chat", "Talk to Founder", "Schedule Chat") ([homepage](https://www.oliv.ai/)).
- **Oliv.ai is the only one with a no-meeting product path**: "Try in Sandbox" in the hero and the final CTA, plus a Pricing nav link and savings calculators ([homepage](https://www.oliv.ai/), [Forecast page](https://www.oliv.ai/oliv-forecast)).
- **Competitive-switch CTAs**: Oliv.ai's banner ("Extorted by Gong? Get Oliv CI free until your contract ends, plus free data migration!") and Spekit's "See the Comparison" both invite a head-to-head evaluation ([Oliv.ai](https://www.oliv.ai/), [Spekit](https://www.spekit.com/)).
- **Content CTAs** carry the rest: Glean routes to event replays, product pages, the Work AI Institute and customer stories; Spekit to its knowledge-engine page, analyst report and tour ([Glean](https://www.glean.com/), [Spekit](https://www.spekit.com/)).

### 6. Storytelling strengths to learn from

- **Oliv.ai**: the maturity ladder ("ChatGPT for all" to "Autonomous agents in production") lets the buyer place their own team before any product claim, and the per-seat price table turns a positioning claim into arithmetic. The close lowers the meeting cost with specifics: "Seven minutes. No pitch." ([homepage](https://www.oliv.ai/)).
- **Glean**: a live-looking proof counter ("Average token savings YTD: 12,442,032,540") and industry tabs that each pair a prompt, an answer image and a named customer quote ([homepage](https://www.glean.com/)).
- **Spekit**: the CRO page opens with a question the buyer has to answer for themselves ("Your product changed this week. Did your reps? Did your agents?") and names a concrete loss, a deal lost to a stale answer ([CRO page](https://www.spekit.com/solutions/chief-revenue-officers)). Its homepage FAQ carries the explicit differentiation and replacement claims, so the hero can stay short ([homepage](https://www.spekit.com/)).

### 7. Messaging opportunities for PitchSense to investigate (updated against earlier batches)

This matches its Phase 1 Shared Memory score of 2. Glean adds a horizontal version. The remaining testable gap is narrower: whether a memory that links what was said on calls to practice and coaching reads as different from Oliv.ai's revenue-ops context graph. Testing question: does "memory" now read as notetaker vocabulary and "context" as platform vocabulary to a CRO? This is a wording test, not a capability claim.
3. **Deal-grounded content with governance (C6.3): now has a direct counterexample.** C6.3 found no hero across the 50 claiming both. Spekit's hero claims "One governed source" covering content creation and deal intelligence, and its CRO page says reps "generate executive summaries, decks, and on-brand content from live deal context, Gong calls, and approved GTM knowledge" and that leaders can see "which content correlates with closed-won deals" ([homepage](https://www.spekit.com/), [CRO page](https://www.spekit.com/solutions/chief-revenue-officers)).
