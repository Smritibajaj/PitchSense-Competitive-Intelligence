Part 1: The story
The problem
Sales teams have more data than ever and still lose deals to things they already knew. The knowledge
exists; it's just scattered and forgotten.
1. Calls disappear. What the buyer said, worried about and asked for lives in a recording nobody
2. 3. 4. 5. 6. 7. 8. 9. replays.
Deals go dark without warning. Risks show up on calls and emails weeks before the forecast.
Nobody connects them in time.
Promises slip. "I'll send that over Thursday" gets lost between back-to-back meetings. The buyer
notices.
Deals move in email and Slack too. A buyer promises a document in an email; a deal update
lands in a Slack thread. None of it reaches the CRM, and nobody notices when it goes quiet.
The right content is never at hand. The answer to the security question is in a deck somewhere.
The rep improvises instead.
Sales material is rebuilt by hand. Reps copy last quarter's proposal, retype what the buyer said,
and drift off brand and off message.
Coaching is guesswork. Managers can listen to a fraction of calls. Feedback comes late and
nobody checks whether it worked.
Knowledge walks out the door. When a rep leaves, their deal history, relationships and context
go with them.
Every tool remembers one thing. A notetaker, a CRM helper, a content tool and a training
platform, each with its own partial memory, none talking to the others.

PitchSense AI turns every sales conversation into intelligence your whole team can use, and never
forgets it. Calls, emails, deals, documents and coaching all draw on one shared memory.

Connecting the dots
PitchSense remembers everything your team hears, says, promises and practices, in one memory your
company owns. Every other part of the product reads it. This is our strongest advantage.
One shared memory matters because of what it lets PitchSense connect. It brings together information
that usually sits in separate tools, so the rep knows what to do before the next call. For example:
The buyer questioned the price on the last call. Your rep tends to defend the price before
asking why. Your company already has a customer example that shows the value.
PitchSense remembers all three and puts them in the pre-call brief, with a 6-minute drill
on handling price.

Why one memory beats many
Other tools remember calls. PitchSense also knows how each rep practises, where they're strong and
weak, and what content the company has. So each part of the product is smarter than it would be alone:
• The pre-call brief knows the buyer's open questions and that this rep struggled with pricing last
time.
• Coaching is based on real deals, not generic skills.
• Content suggestions match the objection the buyer actually raised.
• Proposals and one-pagers are written from what the buyer actually said, in your brand.
• Deal risks come from what was said on every call and email, not only what someone typed into
the CRM.
• Deal status comes from calls, emails and Slack, so nobody reads stale information or updates
anything by hand.
Every answer shows its receipt. Each claim links to its source: the call, the moment and the quote.
Nobody has to take the AI's word for it.
Knowledge stays when people leave. Deal history, promises and buyer context belong to the company,
not to one rep's inbox.

Voice and words
We sound like a sharp sales leader who has seen it all: direct, calm, on the rep's side. Plain words, short
sentences, no hype.
• Direct. Say what it does. "Summarises the call", not "unlocks conversational insights".
• On the rep's side. Reps are the heroes. Never "monitor" or "track your reps".
• Confident, not loud. Contrast the category ("call recorders", "notetakers"), not competitors by
name, in public copy.
• Specific. Minutes, real situations ("a CFO pushing on ROI"), not adjectives.
• Show the receipt. When we claim something, say where it came from. Our product works that
way too.
Sybill is casual and cheeky; Aida is punchy and invents category words ("Digital Twin"). We stand apart
by being calm, clear and checkable.
